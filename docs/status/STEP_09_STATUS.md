# Step 9 — Evaluation and Accessibility

## Delivered behavior

The course directory includes a local device-report download. Learners can record their device RAM, experience and activity; the report includes browser, viewport and current rendering measurements. It does not upload personal information. Use a real device, visit the city, enter a course, complete a level and return to the directory before recording observations.

Registration, the course directory and tutor studio pass automated critical/serious WCAG A/AA checks at 390 px and 1440 px widths. Invalid HUD labels and low-contrast wardrobe text were corrected. Automated checks do not establish complete accessibility; keyboard, screen-reader and disability-specific journeys need human evaluation.

## Recorded evidence

The isolated Node suite passes 58 checks with one optional external PostgreSQL skip. Both new accessibility/device-report browser journeys pass. The cold software-WebGL audit at 390 × 844, 2 Mbps, 100 ms latency and 4× CPU slowdown records 15.45 seconds to playable, 5/9/7 FPS, 1.53 MB transfer and 537 MB browser-process PSS. These measurements vary with runner load and do not prove usability on physical 2 GB hardware. The preceding audit is retained separately for comparison.

## Field evaluation

Test at least one 2 GB phone and one basic laptop, with normal and unreliable connectivity. Record city navigation, text readability, course completion, reconnect behavior, tutor authoring and thermal/battery observations using the local report. Ask learners whether they understood the next activity and why they would return. Preserve anonymized observations with consent; do not claim learning effectiveness from synthetic test accounts.

Physical-device sessions and learner interviews remain unperformed. Offline account operations, shared presence and classification are unavailable without connectivity. Later milestones address classifier integration, trusted practice, portals, advertising and world policy.
